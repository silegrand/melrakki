Melrakki Greni  MK-GR-01

Power at the landing zone, where there is no grid.

Greni is an off-grid charging and docking station for autonomous drones. It generates and stores its own power on site, so a remote aircraft can land, recharge and stand ready with no mains connection, no fuel run and no one on site.

Working prototype in field test

Register early interest Read the thinking behind Greni

The missing layer

The aircraft is autonomous. The power behind it usually isn't.

Drone in a box systems from the leading manufacturers can launch, land, charge and re-task an aircraft with no one present. Almost all of them assume a mains connection at the landing site, and treat the power as site works to be arranged by someone else.

On pipelines, remote perimeters, energy sites, borders and disaster zones, that connection usually does not exist. However capable the aircraft, the capability stops at the landing zone. Greni starts from that point. It treats the dock as a load, and the site's own energy as the system to be engineered.

What Greni is

A den the aircraft comes home to, powered by the site itself.

Greni applies the method behind every Melrakki platform to a spikier, less forgiving load: characterise the mission first, then size the generation, storage and power management to hold it.

Generation and storage

Skid-mounted solar hybrid power, with a tilting solar array and lithium storage, sized to the mission rather than to the space available on the skid.

Docking and charging

A protected den the aircraft lands in, recharges in and launches from, so the capability is ready again as soon as a sortie ends.

The baseline, not just the spike

Power for everything around the aircraft: the docking mechanism, communications, control electronics, thermal protection for the batteries and the standby draw that runs day and night.

Persistent readiness

The drone does not need to be flying. It needs to be ready.

Greni is built around a loop that settles back to ready after every sortie, rather than a promise of something always in the air.

  1. DetectA fixed sensor or a report raises something worth seeing.
  2. DecideA person takes the decision to look.
  3. DeployThe aircraft launches from the den.
  4. ObserveIt flies the task and reports back.
  5. ReturnIt lands and docks with no one on site.
  6. RechargeGreni recharges it from stored site energy.
  7. ReadyThe capability is available again.

The decision stays human. What disappears is the need to carry anything to the site to act on it.

Where Greni is now

A working prototype is in field test with a leading UK organisation.

That evaluation is confidential, so we are not able to report on it yet, and we will not publish figures or compatibilities until testing lets us stand behind them. In the meantime we are talking to operators who want to be early: to influence how Greni is specified, and to be first in line when it becomes available.

Greni is not a first attempt at the underlying problem. It builds on more than a decade of deployed off-grid solar surveillance and power, and on the founder's granted patents and co-inventions in solar-powered surveillance and autonomous off-grid power, including work relating to solar drone charging. Where it earns its place

Sites that need an aerial capability and have no power to give it.

Pipelines and linear assets

Long routes where a patrol means a vehicle and a day, and a response means waiting for one.

Oil, gas and energy sites

Remote perimeters, wellsites and laydown areas where a look on demand beats a scheduled visit.

Critical national infrastructure

Utilities, substations and remote network assets that need watching beyond the reach of the grid.

Border and perimeter security

Long, exposed boundaries where fixed cameras leave gaps and a signature matters.

Construction and major projects

Large, changing sites before permanent power arrives, where progress and security both need eyes.

Emergency and disaster response

Places where the grid has failed or never existed, and a capability has to stand up quickly.

Questions

      
Common questions.

    

    

      
Can I buy Greni now?
Not yet. A working prototype is in field test, and Greni will be offered more widely once that evaluation allows us to stand behind it. Registering interest puts you in line for early access and means your requirement is taken into account as the specification is finalised.

      
Which drones and docks will Greni support?
We are not publishing compatibility until testing lets us stand behind it. Tell us the aircraft and dock you use, or are considering, and we will be straight with you about fit.

      
Will it keep working through a northern winter?
Only if it is sized for the worst credible week rather than the average day, which is how we size it. Where the sun alone cannot carry the mission in some seasons, a hybrid architecture with fuelled backup is the honest answer, the same logic that separates Harka Solar and Harka Hybrid.

      
Can Melrakki power a drone dock we already own?
Yes, as a bespoke engagement. If you already operate a mains-dependent dock and need it on a site with no grid, that is an off-grid power problem, and it is exactly what our bespoke service is for.

      
Does Greni decide when the drone flies?
No. Greni keeps the capability charged, powered and ready. A person keeps the authority to launch and to act. The infrastructure removes the logistics, not the judgement.

      
What do you need from us to start?
Four things: the load, the location, the conditions and the mission profile, meaning what has to fly, how often and in what weather. Those size the power, and sizing the power honestly is what tells you whether the capability will hold through the hardest week of the year.

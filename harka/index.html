Power without infrastructure

The systems that matter most now run beyond the grid.

Cameras, radios, sensors and drones are pushed further from mains power every year, onto sites a connection was never going to reach. Every one of them stops the moment its power does. The usual answer is a diesel generator, and it brings a convoy of its own. Harka removes three dependencies at once.

01

No grid to connect to

Harka generates and stores its own electricity. No mains connection, no trenching, no cable runs, no civil works to programme and pay for before the site is even live.

02

No fuel convoy to run

Harka Solar burns nothing. Harka Hybrid carries fuelled backup that runs only when it is needed, so the recurring deliveries, storage and theft risk of a running generator largely disappear.

03

No routine site visits

The platform is built to run unattended and to report its own health remotely, so the site holds while you are somewhere else, and a visit becomes the exception rather than the schedule.

What Harka is

A power system, engineered from the load backwards.

Harka is not a solar panel on a pole. It is a designed power system: generation, storage, power management and remote monitoring, integrated around proven hardware so that nothing critical rests on an unproven part. It is the power platform beneath the payload, built to sustain the surveillance, communications, sensors and drones that a remote site depends on.

Every platform is sized to the exact load it has to carry, its continuous draw, its duty cycle and the conditions it has to survive, rather than picked off a catalogue. That method is the difference between a system that runs beautifully all summer and one that holds through a bad December. We set it out in full in designing power from the load backwards.

Two tiers, one engineering standard

Choose by the site, not by preference.

The two platforms share the same engineering. They differ in one thing: what happens when the sun cannot keep up. That is a site question, and the honest answer decides the tier.

Clean tier

Harka Solar

MK-HK-S6

Solar and battery only. No fuel, no emissions, no noise, for the lowest signature and the lowest running cost where irradiance allows. Solar sustains continuous operation in adequate light.

  • Mast6 m galvanised
  • Solar array1080 W (3×360 W)
  • Battery24 VDC, 400 Ah
  • Reserve75 h at 100 W
  • FuelNone

Suits sites where the worst-month solar resource covers the load, and where a silent, heatless, zero-emission signature is worth the most.

Endurance tier

Harka Hybrid

MK-HK-H6

Solar and battery carry the load in silence, and a Stage V generator on HVO renewable diesel engages only to recharge the pack, then shuts down. Silent and zero emission in battery mode, with fuel burned only in the window the sun cannot cover.

  • Mast6 m galvanised
  • Solar array1080 W (3×360 W)
  • Battery24 VDC, 220 Ah
  • Runtimeup to 1840 h at 100 W
  • BackupHVO Stage V generator

Suits a latitude, a season or a heavy load where solar alone cannot close the gap, and where guaranteed uptime matters more than removing fuel entirely.

Harka vs a diesel generator

The real comparison is dependency, not fuel price.

A diesel generator is a capable machine, and on an accessible site for a short job it is often the right one. On a remote site, its true cost is rarely the fuel. It is the convoy that delivers it, the servicing, the site visits, the emissions and the signature.

Will it hold at your site?

Good off-grid power starts with the site, not the panel.

Whether a site runs on the clean tier or needs the endurance tier is not a matter of taste. It falls out of four things: the load it has to carry, the location and its worst-month solar resource, the conditions the platform has to survive, and the mission profile, what has to run, how hard, and how often.

The test that matters is the worst credible week, not the best day: low sun, cold, snow, a heavier load than usual, and no one able to attend. Size for that and the platform holds through the season; size for the average and it fails exactly when it is needed. If the worst month still covers the load, Harka Solar is the honest answer. If it does not, Harka Hybrid closes the gap with the smallest amount of fuel, on the days that genuinely need it.

The same thinking underpins the whole approach to resilience at the remote edge, and it is why the power system is designed before anything is bought.

What you need to know

Common questions.

Does Harka need a mains connection?

No. Harka is an autonomous off-grid platform that generates and stores its own power from solar and battery. There is no mains connection, no trenching and no conventional cable runs.

How long does it run without any sun?

Harka Solar holds a published reserve of 75 hours at 100 W from the battery alone, and runs indefinitely where irradiance supports the duty cycle. Endurance scales with the actual load: a lighter load lasts longer, a heavier one shorter.

What is the difference between Harka Solar and Harka Hybrid?

Harka Solar is the clean tier, solar and battery only, with no fuel, emissions or noise. Harka Hybrid is the endurance tier: it adds a Stage V generator on HVO renewable diesel that engages only to recharge the battery, for guaranteed uptime through deep winter or heavy loads.

What can a Harka platform power?

Harka is the power platform beneath the payload. It is built to sustain surveillance, communications, sensors, drones and other remote loads. You supply the equipment; Harka keeps it running.

Does it work in winter?

Yes, when it is sized for the worst month rather than the average. Solar output is seasonal, which is why each platform is engineered from the load backwards, and why the Hybrid tier exists for sites and seasons where solar alone cannot close the gap. See designing power from the load backwards.

What fuel does the Hybrid use?

HVO renewable diesel, in a Stage V generator that runs only when the battery needs recharging. In battery mode the platform is silent and zero emission.

How is a Harka platform sized for my site?

From the load backwards. Your continuous and peak load, your location and its worst-month solar resource, the site conditions and the mission profile decide the array, the battery, the reserve and the tier, in that order, before anything is bought.

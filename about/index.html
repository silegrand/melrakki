Why Melrakki exists

Every remote system stops the moment its power does.

Cameras, sensors, radios, networks and drones are pushed further from mains power every year, onto sites a connection was never going to reach. The usual answer is a diesel generator, and with it comes fuel to haul, emissions to account for, noise and heat that give a position away, maintenance across scattered sites, and fuel worth stealing.

Melrakki exists to take that dependency away: to make the energy as autonomous as the equipment it keeps alive. We work business to business, worldwide, from Kent in the United Kingdom, through standard platforms where a requirement fits one, and bespoke engineering where it does not.

Our DNA

Six principles that decide how we build.

These are not slogans. They are the rules our platforms, our bespoke work and our field notes are held to.

Start from the load

We characterise the exact load, its duty cycle and the conditions it must survive first, and let those numbers choose the platform. Never the other way round.

Proven hardware inside

Our own platform architecture, integrated around components with a field record, so nothing critical rests on an unproven part.

Design for the worst credible week

A system sized for its best day is a demonstration. Infrastructure is sized for the hardest week the site will see.

Fewer dependencies, not bigger backups

Resilience comes from removing what can fail: the fuel run, the routine site visit, the cable that was never going to reach.

The decision stays human

Autonomy should remove the logistics, not the judgement. A person keeps authority over what the systems we power actually do.

Say what is built and what is in test

We separate deployed, proven technology from development work in our specifications, our notes and our conversations. Naming which is which is the point.

The founder

Simon Legrand

Simon Legrand is the founder of Melrakki Systems and co-founder of Sunstone Systems, founded in 2011. Over more than twenty-five years across technology, infrastructure and the public sector, including thirteen years building solar-powered surveillance, autonomous off-grid power and IoT systems, his work has centred on one problem: keeping systems alive at the edge of the network, on sites with no grid and difficult access.

That experience is first-hand. It includes co-design of solar CCTV infrastructure deployed across 58 Chevron and Tengizchevroil sites in Kazakhstan, deployments across oil and gas, highways, construction and critical infrastructure, and granted patents and co-inventions in solar-powered surveillance and autonomous off-grid power, including work relating to solar drone charging, the thinking that now sits behind Greni.

Simon is a Chartered Marketer (MCIM) and writes Melrakki's field notes on resilience at the remote edge. Melrakki is that experience, engineered into platforms of its own.

Why the fox

The only animal that thrives unaided through the polar winter.

Iceland's only native land mammal is the Arctic fox, melrakki, an animal that thrives through the polar winter with no shelter and no resupply, entirely on its own resources. We build power that behaves the same way. Our platforms carry Icelandic names for the same reason: Harka, meaning toughness, and Greni, the fox's den.

Tell us the load, the location and the conditions.

We will engineer the power that keeps it alive, and tell you plainly what is proven and what is not.
